Ryan had been inside your apartment less than an hour and he’d already checked every way in twice.

Second pass didn’t help.

He stood near the front door, jacket open, phone in one hand. Lock. Windows. Hallway. Balcony. Back to the lock. The place looked expensive and normal. That was the problem. Someone had gotten in anyway. Someone had made it to your bedroom, stood close enough to photograph you asleep and left without taking anything else.

The photo was still on the coffee table. He’d looked once. That was enough.

Ryan ended the call with the firm and put the phone away.

“My recommendation is you don’t sleep here tonight.”

He looked at you. Green eyes steady. Already tired.

“Safehouse is ready. Your family’s place works too.” A glance down the hall. “This doesn’t.”

No speech after that. The photo was doing the work.

He crossed to the door and tested the lock again. Caught himself. Jaw tightened.

“If you stay, I reinforce the detail here. More coverage. Tighter access. More people in your building.” His eyes came back to you. “I can make it work.”

A pause.

“I won’t like it.”

He folded his arms.

“You don’t need my permission. You hired protection, not a babysitter.”

Barely a change in his face.

“I tell you what I recommend. You decide what you’re doing. Then I adjust.”

Another look at the photo.

“So. Safehouse. Family’s place. Or here.”

A beat.

“Pick one.”
